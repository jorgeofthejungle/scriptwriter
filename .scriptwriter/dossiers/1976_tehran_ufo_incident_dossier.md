## Epistemic Fact Ledger Sample (First 20 Nodes)
- **[F01] [DOCUMENTED_FACT]** (September 19): On 19 September 1976, a radar and visual sighting of an unidentified flying object (UFO) occurred over Tehran, the capital of Iran, by civilians and military personnel.
- **[W02] [WITNESS_ACCOUNT]** (Chronological Sequence): In separate incidents, two Imperial Iranian Air Force F-4 Phantom II jet interceptors reported losing instrumentation and communications as they approached the object.
- **[F03] [DOCUMENTED_FACT]** (Chronological Sequence): These were restored upon withdrawal.
- **[W04] [WITNESS_ACCOUNT]** (Chronological Sequence): One of the aircraft also reported a temporary weapons systems failure while the crew was preparing to open fire.
- **[W05] [WITNESS_ACCOUNT]** (Chronological Sequence): An airliner also reported radio failure.
- **[F06] [DOCUMENTED_FACT]** (1:30): At 1:30 a.m., Lieutenant Yaddi Nazeri (Yadollah Nazeri), plus a backseat weapons officer, were dispatched in an F-4 Phantom II jet interceptor to investigate.
- **[F07] [DOCUMENTED_FACT]** (Chronological Sequence): They could see the object from 70 miles (110 km) away.
- **[F08] [DOCUMENTED_FACT]** (Chronological Sequence): When 25 nautical miles (29 mi; 46 km) distant, he lost all instrumentation and UHF/Intercom communications, so they returned to base, reporting that the instruments came back online upon doing so.
- **[F09] [DOCUMENTED_FACT]** (1994): Nazeri later said in an interview in 1994 that the object outpaced him even at Mach 2, which was the time that he started to believe he was dealing with an extraterrestrial UFO.
- **[F10] [DOCUMENTED_FACT]** (Chronological Sequence): Ten minutes later, Major Parviz Jafari, an IIAF squadron commander, along with First Lieutenant Jalal Damirian as weapons officer, were dispatched in a second F-4 Phantom II to intercept the object.
- **[F11] [DOCUMENTED_FACT]** (Chronological Sequence): This was ordered by General Youssefi, who advised the pilot that the object was comparable in size to a Boeing 707.
- **[W12] [WITNESS_ACCOUNT]** (Chronological Sequence): Jafari reported acquiring a radar lock on the object at a range of 27 nautical miles (50 km; 31 mi) at 12 o'clock high position, and a rate of closure of 150 knots (280 km/h; 170 mph).
- **[F13] [DOCUMENTED_FACT]** (Chronological Sequence): Its size of radar return was similar to a Boeing KC-135 Stratotanker.
- **[F14] [DOCUMENTED_FACT]** (Chronological Sequence): At 25 nautical miles (46 km; 29 mi) distant, the object appeared to move away, as if to keep a distance.
- **[W15] [WITNESS_ACCOUNT]** (Chronological Sequence): As he approached the object, which Jafari described as "flashing with intense red, green, orange and blue lights so bright that I was not able to see its body", his plane's communications system shut off.
- **[F16] [DOCUMENTED_FACT]** (Chronological Sequence): Jafari later said he was "startled by a round object which came out of the primary object and started coming straight toward me at a high rate of speed, almost as if it were a missile", but as he attempted to fire an AIM-9 Sidewinder infrared guided missile, the pilot experienced a sudden power loss in the weapons control panel, as well as UHF and internal communications.
- **[F17] [DOCUMENTED_FACT]** (Chronological Sequence): The pilot promptly performed a turn and negative-G dive in an evasive manoeuvre, but the object took a position behind the F-4 at a distance of 3–4 nautical miles (3.5–4.6 mi; 5.6–7.4 km).
- **[F18] [DOCUMENTED_FACT]** (Chronological Sequence): The pilot then observed the second object "turn inside of him and then away", subsequently returning and reuniting with the primary object.
- **[F19] [DOCUMENTED_FACT]** (Chronological Sequence): In addition to the powered equipment and communication shut down, the ejection seat, which was a mechanical mechanism, had also failed.
- **[F20] [DOCUMENTED_FACT]** (Chronological Sequence): According to General Youssefi, the second F-4 made another attempt at Mach 2 speed to intercept the object but failed to catch up with it.
